# Sources and further reading

The sources below support the **three available sample chapters**. Each annotation states what the source contributes and where its use stops. This is a selected reading list, not a systematic review of the entire proposed book. Chapter links place the evidence beside the claims it supports.

## Interpreting behaviour and checking your own judgment

**Gilbert, D. T., & Malone, P. S. (1995).** The correspondence bias. *Psychological Bulletin, 117*(1), 21–38. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7870861/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.117.1.21.

A conceptual review used in [Chapter 1](chapters/01-before-you-explain.md) to explain why an action need not reveal an enduring disposition. It does not show that every judgment about character is wrong.

**Nickerson, R. S. (1998).** Confirmation bias: A ubiquitous phenomenon in many guises. *Review of General Psychology, 2*(2), 175–220. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175).

A review of selective information seeking and interpretation. Supports the account of confirmation bias; the Rowan scenario is an illustration, not a finding from this paper.

**Lord, C. G., Lepper, M. R., & Preston, E. (1984).** Considering the opposite: A corrective strategy for social judgment. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 47*(6), 1231–1243. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6527215/). DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.47.6.1231.

Two experiments supporting a bounded claim about considering alternatives in social-judgment tasks. The book's conversational exercise is an application, not the tested procedure or a validated relationship intervention.

**Malle, B. F. (2006).** The actor–observer asymmetry in attribution: A (surprising) meta-analysis. *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(6), 895–919. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073526/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.895.

A synthesis of 173 studies challenging a general actor–observer asymmetry. Included to qualify a familiar psychological slogan, rather than presenting it as an established universal rule.

## Motivation, opportunity and action

**Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2020).** Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation from a self-determination theory perspective: Definitions, theory, practices, and future directions. *Contemporary Educational Psychology, 61*, 101860. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2020.101860); [author-hosted article](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2020_RyanDeci_IntrinsicandExtrinsic.pdf).

Theoretical and research review underpinning the motivation distinctions and three psychological needs in [Chapter 3](chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.md). Its educational focus is identified when the book extends those questions to volunteering.

**Howard, J. L., Bureau, J., Guay, F., Chong, J. X. Y., & Ryan, R. M. (2021).** Student motivation and associated outcomes: A meta-analysis from self-determination theory. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 16*(6), 1300–1323. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33593153/). DOI: 10.1177/1745691620966789.

Synthesises associations between types of motivation and student outcomes. Used to distinguish personal value from enjoyment, with explicit limits on causal inference and transfer to the garden example.

**Webb, T. L., & Sheeran, P. (2006).** Does changing behavioral intentions engender behavior change? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence. *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(2), 249–268. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16536643/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249.

Forty-seven experimental tests support distinguishing changes in intention from changes in action. They do not identify why a particular person's plan failed.

**Wilson, J. (2000).** Volunteering. *Annual Review of Sociology, 26*, 215–240. [Author's university record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1312130). DOI: 10.1146/annurev.soc.26.1.215.

A sociological review that brings social ties, organisations and resources into the explanation of volunteering, alongside individual motives. Used to widen the questions asked about participation.

**Wilson, J. (2012).** Volunteerism research: A review essay. *Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly, 41*(2), 176–212. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1177/0899764011434558).

Reviews antecedents, experiences and consequences of volunteering, largely through survey research. Identifies gaps in understanding the social experience of volunteering; it is not causal evidence that a particular organisational change will improve attendance.

**Hagger, M. S., & Luszczynska, A. (2014).** Implementation intention and action planning interventions in health contexts: State of the research and proposals for the way forward. *Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being, 6*(1), 1–47. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24591064/). DOI: 10.1111/aphw.12017.

Reviews planning interventions, their varied effects and methodological limitations. The garden's if–then plan is a practical adaptation outside the review's health-behaviour setting.

## Cultural coverage

**Henrich, J., Heine, S. J., & Norenzayan, A. (2010).** The weirdest people in the world? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 33*(2–3), 61–83. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20550733/). DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X0999152X.

A review and critique motivating scrutiny of research populations and cultural generalisation. It does not licence inferences about an individual's character from cultural membership.

## Deception judgments and investigative interviewing

**Bond, C. F., Jr., & DePaulo, B. M. (2006).** Accuracy of deception judgments. *Personality and Social Psychology Review, 10*(3), 214–234. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/). DOI: 10.1207/s15327957pspr1003_2.

Supports the overall and separate truth–lie accuracy figures in [Chapter 9](chapters/09-truth-lies-uncertainty.md). These are averages from unaided judgment tasks, not probabilities about an individual speaker.

**Vrij, A., Hartwig, M., & Granhag, P. A. (2019).** Reading lies: Nonverbal communication and deception. *Annual Review of Psychology, 70*, 295–317. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30609913/). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-103135.

Review supporting caution about nonverbal deception cues. It challenges confident interpretations of demeanour, not the value of all nonverbal communication.

**High-Value Detainee Interrogation Group (2016).** *Interrogation: A Review of the Science.* September 2016. [Official FBI report page](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/reports-and-publications/hig-report-interrogation-a-review-of-the-science-september-2016.pdf/view).

A research synthesis from the FBI-administered HIG, used for its discussion of information gathering and rapport. Its investigative settings limit direct transfer into everyday relationships. It does not validate everything described as an FBI technique.

## Support guidance

**National Institute of Mental Health.** [Caring for your mental health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health). Public guidance on seeking professional help, including distress and difficulty with usual activities. Its service information is US-specific.

**NHS.** [Domestic violence and abuse](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/getting-help-for-domestic-violence/). Public guidance on recognising abuse and obtaining support, including digital-safety considerations. Its services are UK-specific. These guidance pages inform [When this book is the wrong tool](support.md); they are separate from research claims about everyday behaviour.

## Research to develop next

The planned chapters on personality, emotion, culture, power, conflict and cooperation need their own source records. Development should include relevant field and qualitative research, newer syntheses, contrary findings and corrections, rather than extending the claims above to cover those subjects by implication.
