# Sources and further reading

The sources below support the **twelve chapters**. Each annotation states what the source contributes and where its use stops. This is a selected reading list, not a systematic review of the entire field. Chapter links place the evidence beside the claims it supports. Research records and bibliographic details for this draft were checked on 27 October 2026; that check does not make older findings the latest evidence on a topic.

## Interpreting behaviour and checking your own judgment

**Gilbert, D. T., & Malone, P. S. (1995).** The correspondence bias. *Psychological Bulletin, 117*(1), 21–38. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7870861/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.117.1.21.

A conceptual review used in [Chapter 1](chapters/01-before-you-explain.md) to explain why an action need not reveal an enduring disposition. It does not show that every judgment about character is wrong.

**Nickerson, R. S. (1998).** Confirmation bias: A ubiquitous phenomenon in many guises. *Review of General Psychology, 2*(2), 175–220. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.2.175).

A review of selective information seeking and interpretation. Supports the account of confirmation bias; the Rowan scenario is an illustration, not a finding from this paper.

**Lord, C. G., Lepper, M. R., & Preston, E. (1984).** Considering the opposite: A corrective strategy for social judgment. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 47*(6), 1231–1243. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6527215/). DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.47.6.1231.

Two experiments supporting a bounded claim about considering alternatives in social-judgment tasks. The book's conversational exercise is an application, not the tested procedure or a validated relationship intervention.

**Malle, B. F. (2006).** The actor–observer asymmetry in attribution: A (surprising) meta-analysis. *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(6), 895–919. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073526/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.895.

A synthesis of 173 studies challenging a general actor–observer asymmetry. Included to qualify a familiar psychological slogan, rather than presenting it as an established universal rule.

## Motivation, opportunity and action

**Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2020).** Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation from a self-determination theory perspective: Definitions, theory, practices, and future directions. *Contemporary Educational Psychology, 61*, 101860. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2020.101860); [author-hosted article](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2020_RyanDeci_IntrinsicandExtrinsic.pdf).

Theoretical and research review underpinning the motivation distinctions and three psychological needs in [Chapter 3](chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.md). Its educational focus is identified when the book extends those questions to volunteering.

**Howard, J. L., Bureau, J., Guay, F., Chong, J. X. Y., & Ryan, R. M. (2021).** Student motivation and associated outcomes: A meta-analysis from self-determination theory. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 16*(6), 1300–1323. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33593153/). DOI: 10.1177/1745691620966789.

Synthesises associations between types of motivation and student outcomes. Used to distinguish personal value from enjoyment, with explicit limits on causal inference and transfer to the garden example.

**Webb, T. L., & Sheeran, P. (2006).** Does changing behavioral intentions engender behavior change? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence. *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(2), 249–268. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16536643/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249.

Forty-seven experimental tests support distinguishing changes in intention from changes in action. They do not identify why a particular person's plan failed.

**Wilson, J. (2000).** Volunteering. *Annual Review of Sociology, 26*, 215–240. [Author's university record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1312130). DOI: 10.1146/annurev.soc.26.1.215.

A sociological review that brings social ties, organisations and resources into the explanation of volunteering, alongside individual motives. Used to widen the questions asked about participation.

**Wilson, J. (2012).** Volunteerism research: A review essay. *Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly, 41*(2), 176–212. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1177/0899764011434558).

Reviews antecedents, experiences and consequences of volunteering, largely through survey research. Identifies gaps in understanding the social experience of volunteering; it is not causal evidence that a particular organisational change will improve attendance.

**Hagger, M. S., & Luszczynska, A. (2014).** Implementation intention and action planning interventions in health contexts: State of the research and proposals for the way forward. *Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being, 6*(1), 1–47. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24591064/). [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12017).

Reviews planning interventions, their varied effects and methodological limitations. The garden's if–then plan is a practical adaptation outside the review's health-behaviour setting.

## Cultural coverage

**Henrich, J., Heine, S. J., & Norenzayan, A. (2010).** The weirdest people in the world? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 33*(2–3), 61–83. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20550733/). DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X0999152X.

A review and critique motivating scrutiny of research populations and cultural generalisation. It does not licence inferences about an individual's character from cultural membership.

## Deception judgments and investigative interviewing

**Bond, C. F., Jr., & DePaulo, B. M. (2006).** Accuracy of deception judgments. *Personality and Social Psychology Review, 10*(3), 214–234. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/). DOI: 10.1207/s15327957pspr1003_2.

Supports the overall and separate truth–lie accuracy figures in [Chapter 9](chapters/09-truth-lies-uncertainty.md). These are averages from unaided judgment tasks, not probabilities about an individual speaker.

**Vrij, A., Hartwig, M., & Granhag, P. A. (2019).** Reading lies: Nonverbal communication and deception. *Annual Review of Psychology, 70*, 295–317. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30609913/). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-103135.

Review supporting caution about nonverbal deception cues. It challenges confident interpretations of demeanour, not the value of all nonverbal communication.

**High-Value Detainee Interrogation Group (2016).** *Interrogation: A Review of the Science.* September 2016. [Official FBI report page](https://www.fbi.gov/file-repository/reports-and-publications/hig-report-interrogation-a-review-of-the-science-september-2016.pdf/view).

A research synthesis from the FBI-administered HIG, used for its discussion of information gathering and rapport. Its investigative settings limit direct transfer into everyday relationships. It does not validate everything described as an FBI technique.

## Evidence, probability and causal reasoning

**Rohrer, J. M. (2018).** Thinking clearly about correlations and causation: Graphical causal models for observational data. *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science, 1*(1), 27–42. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245917745629).

A methodological account of causal assumptions, confounding and statistical control. [Chapter 2](chapters/02-what-counts-as-knowing.md) uses it to distinguish an observed association from the effect of changing an arrangement; the neighbourhood example is an original illustration.

**Gigerenzer, G., & Hoffrage, U. (1995).** How to improve Bayesian reasoning without instruction: Frequency formats. *Psychological Review, 102*(4), 684–704. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.4.684); [research record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ517158).

Experimental work on frequency representations in reasoning tasks. Supports trying counts when interpreting probabilities. Chapter 2's screening numbers are invented to demonstrate arithmetic, not drawn from this study or a real test.

## Beliefs and identity

**Kunda, Z. (1990).** The case for motivated reasoning. *Psychological Bulletin, 108*(3), 480–498. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2270237/). DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.108.3.480.

A review of how goals can influence reasoning and the constraints on reaching preferred conclusions. [Chapter 4](chapters/04-beliefs-identity-history.md) uses it to prompt scrutiny of one's own stake in an answer, without inferring a hidden motive from disagreement.

**McAdams, D. P., & McLean, K. C. (2013).** Narrative identity. *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 22*(3). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721413475622).

A review of evolving life stories, meaning and development. It identifies unresolved causal and cultural questions. Chapter 4 uses the concept to invite a person's account, rather than reconstructing their history from behaviour.

## Emotion, stress and coping

**Shields, G. S., Sazma, M. A., & Yonelinas, A. P. (2016).** The effects of acute stress on core executive functions: A meta-analysis and comparison with cortisol. *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 68*, 651–668. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27371161/). DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.06.038.

Synthesises effects on working memory, cognitive flexibility and inhibition, with variation across conditions. [Chapter 5](chapters/05-emotion-stress-coping.md) keeps cognitive-task findings separate from explanations of an individual's sharp response.

**Bonanno, G. A., & Burton, C. L. (2013).** Regulatory flexibility: An individual differences perspective on coping and emotion regulation. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 8*(6), 591–612. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173226/). DOI: 10.1177/1745691613504116.

A theoretical review proposing context sensitivity, a repertoire of strategies and response to feedback. Used to question universal prescriptions for coping; the chapter's suggested adjustments are practical applications.

## Personality and habits

**Soto, C. J., & John, O. P. (2017).** The next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and assessing a hierarchical model with 15 facets to enhance bandwidth, fidelity, and predictive power. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 113*(1), 117–143. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27055049/). DOI: 10.1037/pspp0000096.

Development and validation of a particular dimensional personality measure. [Chapter 6](chapters/06-personality-habits-situations.md) uses its domains and facets to explain dimensional description; the book does not administer or score the inventory. The record notes an erratum correcting references to McCrae and Costa.

**Fleeson, W. (2001).** Toward a structure- and process-integrated view of personality: Traits as density distributions of states. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 80*(6), 1011–1027. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11414368/). DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.80.6.1011.

Three experience-sampling studies showing substantial within-person variation alongside stable average differences over the studied weeks. Supports considering tendencies and situations together, without claiming that brief observations establish a lifelong pattern.

**Wood, W., & Rünger, D. (2016).** Psychology of habit. *Annual Review of Psychology, 67*, 289–314. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/). DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417.

A review of contextual repetition, automaticity and the relationship between habits and goals. The reading-routine example is an application, not a tested protocol or a claim that all repeated behaviour is habitual.

## Culture, institutions and anthropology

**Vignoles, V. L., and colleagues (2016).** Beyond the “East–West” dichotomy: Global variation in cultural models of selfhood. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 145*(8), 966–1000. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27359126/). DOI: 10.1037/xge0000175.

Two multinational surveys challenging a single independence–interdependence contrast. [Chapter 7](chapters/07-culture-groups-power.md) uses the findings to qualify broad cultural categories, not to predict an individual's conduct from group membership.

**Lareau, A. (2002).** Invisible inequality: Social class and childrearing in Black families and white families. *American Sociological Review, 67*(5). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/000312240206700507).

Situated ethnographic evidence about family practices and interactions with institutions in the United States. Supports attention to processes of advantage; it does not establish universal family types or test the chapter's residents' meeting changes.

**Henrich, J., and colleagues (2005).** “Economic man” in cross-cultural perspective: Behavioral experiments in 15 small-scale societies. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 28*(6). [Author's university record](https://henrich.fas.harvard.edu/publications/economic-man-cross-cultural-perspective-behavioral-experiments-15-small-scale).

Cross-cultural economic games interpreted with local social and economic context. Supports widening the populations and meanings considered in accounts of cooperation. Group associations do not establish causal effects of institutional change or rank cultures by moral worth.

## Questions, listening and memory

**Eyal, T., Steffel, M., & Epley, N. (2018).** Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 114*(4), 547–571. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/). DOI: 10.1037/pspa0000115.

A series of experiments distinguishing imagined perspective-taking from obtaining information through conversation. [Chapter 8](chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.md) preserves the limits of the accuracy tasks and does not treat an answer as guaranteed access to private thoughts.

**Itzchakov, G., Kluger, A. N., & Castro, D. R. (2017).** I am aware of my inconsistencies but can tolerate them: The effect of high quality listening on speakers' attitude ambivalence. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 43*(1), 105–120. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27856728/). DOI: 10.1177/0146167216675339.

Four laboratory experiments on listening, social anxiety and attitude structure. Used for a bounded account of listening, without promising agreement or relationship repair.

**Loftus, E. F. (2005).** Planting misinformation in the human mind: A 30-year investigation of the malleability of memory. *Learning & Memory, 12*(4), 361–366. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16027179/). DOI: 10.1101/lm.94705.

A review of post-event misinformation and memory reports. Supports care with leading questions; it does not establish that a particular recollection is false or justify dismissing an account of harm.

## Supporting performance and reviewing action

**Cerasoli, C. P., Nicklin, J. M., & Ford, M. T. (2014).** Intrinsic motivation and extrinsic incentives jointly predict performance: A 40-year meta-analysis. *Psychological Bulletin, 140*(4), 980–1008. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24491020/). DOI: 10.1037/a0035661.

Synthesises relationships involving motivation, incentives and performance quality and quantity. [Chapter 10](chapters/10-motivation-influence-cooperation.md) uses the distinctions to resist blanket claims about rewards; it does not prescribe the fictional group's incentive design.

**Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013).** Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques: Promising directions from cognitive and educational psychology. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14*(1), 4–58. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/). DOI: 10.1177/1529100612453266.

A review of ten techniques supporting the discussion of practice testing and distributed practice. The study example is a practical adaptation; the chapter does not extend this evidence directly to musical rehearsal or guarantee grades.

**Harkin, B., and colleagues (2016).** Does monitoring goal progress promote goal attainment? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence. *Psychological Bulletin, 142*(2), 198–229. [Research record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/). DOI: 10.1037/bul0000025.

A synthesis of 138 experiments supporting progress monitoring on average. [Chapter 12](chapters/12-putting-understanding-to-work.md) distinguishes that finding from its household example and the book's unvalidated combined framework.

## Gaslighting and harmful control

**Sweet, P. L. (2019).** The sociology of gaslighting. *American Sociological Review, 84*(5), 851–875. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122419874843).

A qualitative and theoretical analysis using domestic violence to examine how gaslighting operates through power and social inequalities. [Chapter 11](chapters/11-conflict-manipulation-boundaries.md) uses it to explain a process, not to diagnose a person or classify every disputed memory as abuse.

## Support guidance

**National Institute of Mental Health.** [Caring for your mental health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health). Public guidance on seeking professional help, including distress and difficulty with usual activities. Its service information is US-specific.

**NHS.** [Domestic violence and abuse](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/getting-help-for-domestic-violence/). Public guidance on recognising abuse and obtaining support, including digital-safety considerations. Its services are UK-specific. These guidance pages inform [When this book is the wrong tool](support.md); they are separate from research claims about everyday behaviour.

## Keeping the evidence accountable

The list combines foundational accounts and later research rather than claiming exhaustive coverage. Future revisions should consider newer syntheses, field and qualitative evidence, failed replications, corrections and challenges to the claims used here. A source's inclusion does not validate the book's full framework or every suggested exercise. Specific corrections can be raised through the [repository](https://github.com/snowch/understanding-people/issues).
