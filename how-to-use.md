# How to use this book

Read with a situation in mind, including one involving your own behaviour. The aim is to leave with a better question or a more considered action, rather than a label for a person.

## Follow the explanation, then try it

The main text develops an idea through research and worked examples. Green panels headed **Try this** or **Put it into practice** turn that discussion into a small task. Their wording and checklist icon identify them in print and for readers who do not distinguish colours.

You can read straight through without doing every exercise. When a question fits your situation, pause and use it. Avoid turning ordinary interactions into a running assessment of everyone around you.

<section class="reader-action" markdown="1" aria-labelledby="choose-example">

### Try this: choose an example you can revisit {#choose-example}

Pick a recent, low-stakes event. Write what you observed, your current explanation and one thing you do not yet know. After reading a chapter, return to those notes: what would you now ask or do differently?

If you are thinking about someone else, include **your own part in the interaction**. If you are thinking about yourself, include the circumstances as well as your intentions.

## Know what each element means

Outlined boxes labelled **Definition** explain unfamiliar terms in plain English and give an example. Select the term to find it in the alphabetical [glossary](glossary.md). Each glossary entry links back to its explanation in a chapter.

Research claims have nearby source links and a description of the relevant limits. Possible explanations are presented as possibilities. Suggested conversations and action panels are practical applications unless a passage explicitly describes a tested intervention. See the [evidence standards](evidence.md) for more detail.

All named scenarios are fictional teaching examples. They show how an explanation might develop; they are not case-study evidence that an approach succeeds.

The five-step framework and worksheet are the book's synthesis. They have not been validated as a combined assessment or intervention. This book offers education, not diagnosis, treatment, a personality test or a way to establish guilt. Where distress, impairment or safety is the central concern, use the [support page](support.md) to consider a more appropriate next step.

## Use uncertainty without getting stuck

You can make a proportionate decision before understanding someone's motives completely. A repeated missed commitment may justify changing an arrangement. You can acknowledge that an explanation is uncertain while being clear about the effect of the behaviour.

Likewise, an alternative explanation does not have to be a reassuring one. The task is to consider plausible accounts and check them fairly, including unwelcome evidence. There is no obligation to keep searching for a benign interpretation of harmful conduct.

## Read the current edition as a draft

All [twelve chapters](contents.md) are available as a complete first draft. Each closes with **Key takeaways**, followed by a practical exercise. This edition has not undergone external subject-matter review, and its source list is selective rather than a systematic review of all relevant research.

The most useful feedback identifies a passage: what claim seems unsupported, what explanation needs clarification, or what question the example leaves unanswered. Sources and revision history are available through the page-source links.
