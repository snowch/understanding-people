Sometimes the most useful question is not why someone is doing this. It is what help is needed, or what would make the situation safer. Understanding behaviour can support a decision, but it need not come before care or protection.

For immediate danger or an urgent risk of serious harm, contact emergency services where you are. Do not wait to finish this recording, identify a diagnosis or prove a motive. A general book cannot assess individual safety or replace advice suited to your circumstances.

Withdrawal, missed commitments and difficult interactions can have different explanations. A brief account cannot settle whether somebody has depression or a personality disorder. Describe what changed and its practical effect, rather than treating the book as a symptom checklist.

The United States National Institute of Mental Health recommends professional help for severe or distressing symptoms lasting two weeks or more, including difficulties with usual activities. That is not a requirement to wait. Seek advice earlier when something worries you, and act promptly on an immediate safety concern.

For yourself, a primary care clinician or qualified mental health professional can help assess what is happening. With someone else, you can describe what you noticed and offer help finding support without proposing a diagnosis. Respect their choices where there is no immediate safety emergency.

If it is safe to make notes, record what changed, when it began and how it affects daily life. Include what you want help with. You do not have to arrive at a professional conversation with a label already chosen.

A supportive opening might be, you seem to be having a hard time and you mentioned struggling with everyday tasks. Would help finding someone to talk to be useful? This fictional wording invites a response. It cannot guarantee agreement or replace urgent help when that is needed.

If someone threatens you, controls access to money or support, or makes you afraid of their reaction, confronting them to test an explanation can increase risk. Consider confidential advice from an appropriate local domestic-abuse service and a safety plan suited to your situation. You do not have to wait for an emergency.

The written page links to NHS guidance for the United Kingdom. Readers elsewhere need services in their own country. If a device or browsing may be monitored, consider a safer way to contact support. Private-browsing mode does not make activity invisible; use advice about digital safety appropriate to the circumstances.

Legal disputes, safeguarding concerns and high-stakes medical or financial decisions may require an appropriate professional or formal process. A conversation technique cannot establish the facts those decisions need. The clinical and abuse-related material in this book has not received specialist review.

For ordinary decisions, the worksheet can help identify what you know, what remains uncertain and what you can reasonably do. But more analysis is not always the next step. Seek the kind of help the decision requires, and let care or protection proceed without waiting for a complete explanation.
