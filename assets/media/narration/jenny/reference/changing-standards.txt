Moving the goalposts is an everyday phrase for changing a standard after someone has met it or begun meeting it. Such changes can make completion hard to evaluate. The phrase describes something to investigate, not a diagnosis of the person changing the requirement.

New information, an overlooked risk or an incomplete first agreement may justify a revision. A change might also add work without renegotiation or avoid acknowledging that an earlier condition was satisfied. Your own assumption that finished had a shared meaning can contribute to confusion.
