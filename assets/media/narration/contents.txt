Understanding people. Contents and reading routes.

The chapters follow a shared process of understanding people. Family, work, education, friendship, public life and purchasing decisions provide examples throughout; they do not define separate audiences or sections.

Draft status: all twelve chapters are available. This is a complete first draft, with concepts explained before brief supporting examples, linked research, practical exercises and chapter takeaways. It has not undergone external subject-matter review.

Part I · Seeing clearly.

Why we misinterpret people — Separate what you saw or heard from why you think it happened, and check other possible explanations.

What counts as knowing? — Decide what a study, a number or an observation can tell you—and what would change your mind.

Part II · What shapes behaviour?

Needs, motives and competing goals — Explore the difference between wanting to do something and being able to do it, by separating reasons, practical conditions and competing aims, then applying the distinctions to everyday plans.

Beliefs, identity and personal history — Expectations, self-image, values, personal history and why the same event can mean different things to different people.

Emotion, stress and coping — Explore how stress can affect thinking and why looking calm does not tell us how someone feels.

Personality, habits and situations — Understand personality as a set of tendencies, notice how situations change behaviour, and see how habits develop.

Culture, groups and power — Ask what a group expects, who makes the decisions and who can afford to disagree. Learn about culture without assuming everyone in a group is alike.

Part III · Learning about another person.

Questions, listening and disagreement — Ask clear questions, listen to the answer and check whether you understood it. Learn why memory and imagination can mislead us.

Truth, lies and uncertainty — Examine what research says about spotting lies, checking evidence and techniques described as “FBI methods”.

Part IV · Choosing a response.

Motivation, influence and cooperation — Supporting action, explaining value, negotiating and respecting choice. Applications across learning, leadership, shared projects and buying decisions.

Conflict, manipulation and boundaries — Recognise repeated harm, understand gaslighting and controlling behaviour, and set limits without needing to diagnose anyone.

Putting understanding to work — Bring the book's questions together, try a small change you can undo, and recognise when more analysis will not help.

Conclusion.

What to take with you — Revisit the main lessons, try one fresh situation, and choose a question to use during the week.
