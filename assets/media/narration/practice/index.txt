Practice in this book means trying a useful distinction in an ordinary situation, then returning to what happened. You do not need an app or an AI account. The aim is a better explanation or a more workable response, not a score for how well you can classify people.

Choose something small enough to examine fairly and safe enough to consider. For a concern about coercion, harm or serious distress, use the support page first. You do not have to create another incident or confront someone to make an exercise count.

The situation worksheet gives you a sequence. Describe what happened before explaining why. Compare a plausible alternative, choose a question or check, and decide what you can do. After taking that step, review what changed and what remains unknown.

Use the same situation throughout. Switching between several memories can make each stage seem to support your preferred story. Write what you know first-hand, distinguish exact words from a remembered general meaning, and keep your interpretation separate from both.

If you are collecting only examples that fit a belief, try the confirmation-bias practice. Decide what would weaken the claim and use the same standard for supporting and challenging information. If an action has become a broad character judgment, the correspondence-bias practice helps you examine the circumstances.

The declining-help practice asks you to compare offers, not just refusals. What differed about the task, timing, decisions or expected obligations? A useful outcome may be clearer support or a respected no. It need not be persuading the person to accept your offer.

Which single distinction would change your next question or action? Name it before choosing an exercise. You can pause the player to decide. There is no requirement to finish every exercise or choose the most difficult situation.
