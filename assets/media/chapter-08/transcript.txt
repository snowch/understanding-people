0:00 — A different problem than expected
Imani and Rosa are planning a holiday. Imani wants to book early, but Rosa keeps asking for more time. Before giving a speech about indecision, Imani asks what Rosa still needs to decide. Rosa explains that food and transport may make the whole trip unaffordable. The issue was not choosing a destination, as Imani had imagined.

0:22 — Get information you do not have
Eyal and colleagues tested whether imagining another person’s perspective improved accuracy. Across twenty-five experiments, it did not consistently help. In a final experiment, talking to the person did help. That supports obtaining information rather than polishing a guess. It does not guarantee that every answer is complete or truthful, or require someone to disclose private matters.

0:48 — Start with room to explain
An open question invites someone to explain in their own words rather than simply answer yes or no. What is making the decision difficult leaves more room than why are you being difficult. After Rosa identifies cost, Imani can ask whether a total budget would help. Rosa can state what she can spend without revealing her income or debts.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.html#start-open-then-make-the-uncertainty-specific

1:10 — Check what you heard
Imani could say: You like the idea, but food and transport might make it too expensive. Have I understood you correctly? Rosa can correct that account. So you want everyone to wait because you did not plan adds blame instead. Imani can explain her own worry directly: I am concerned that prices will rise if we wait.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.html#listen-for-correction-not-just-your-turn-to-speak

1:32 — Open can still be leading
A leading question suggests the answer the questioner expects. Why did you break your promise is open, but it assumes a promise was broken. If the friends disagree about an earlier agreement, what do you remember us agreeing leaves more room. Separate the remembered words from what each person understood them to mean.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.html#keep-memory-questions-free-of-unnecessary-suggestion

1:52 — Check a record without forcing a story
Elizabeth Loftus reviewed research showing that misleading information after an event can affect later memory. That is a reason not to supply unverified details in questions. It does not prove a particular memory false or justify dismissing harm. Where an appropriate message exists, check it together. I would love to come may express interest rather than a booking commitment.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.html#keep-memory-questions-free-of-unnecessary-suggestion

2:17 — Identify the disagreement
The friends might disagree about what the hotel price includes, what waiting might cost, or what each person wants. Check the quote for included costs. Discuss uncertainty about later prices. Negotiate different preferences. Explaining the hotel bill cannot decide how much Rosa should spend. Each kind of disagreement calls for a different next step.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.html#find-the-layer-of-disagreement

2:42 — Let the answer change the plan
Imani can explain a real deadline: I need to book by Friday. Could we compare the full costs tonight? If it does not fit your budget, I can book my trip and we can plan something else together. Rosa may still decline. Once she understands the options and answers, repeated questions aimed at changing her no can become pressure.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.html#know-when-to-stop-asking

3:03 — One question to remember
After someone explains a concern, pause and ask: Have I understood you correctly? Let them correct you before giving your view. Afterwards, notice whether you learned something or merely secured agreement. If asking could lead to threats or harm, you do not have to continue. Better wording cannot make an unsafe relationship safe on its own.
