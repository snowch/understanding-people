0:00 — Leila wants to take part
Leila signs up to help at a community garden. She wants to meet people and spend time outdoors. But on Saturday she is arranging a care visit for her father, misses the bus, and stays home. After another missed session, both she and the organiser wonder whether she is simply not motivated enough.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#wanting-choosing-and-doing-are-different-questions

0:19 — Wanting, opportunity and action
Separate wanting to do something from having a workable opportunity and following through. Does Leila still want to come? Can the care visit, journey and session fit together? Does she know where to go and what to do if delayed? A motivational speech cannot change the bus timetable. Equally, transport will not create an interest she does not have.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#wanting-choosing-and-doing-are-different-questions

0:43 — More than one reason
Leila might enjoy growing plants, value helping the neighbourhood, want company, and feel guilty about disappointing people. These reasons can coexist. Intrinsic motivation means doing an activity because the activity itself is enjoyable or interesting. Extrinsic motivation means doing it for a result beyond the activity. That result can matter personally; it is not always pressure or a prize.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#what-kind-of-reason-is-moving-you

1:10 — Choice, ability and connection
Ryan and Deci’s paper on self-determination theory in education discusses autonomy, competence and relatedness. In ordinary language, these concern feeling that an action is your choice, feeling able to make progress, and feeling connected to others. We can use those ideas to ask about Leila’s experience. They do not tell us her answer in advance.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#does-the-activity-offer-choice-confidence-and-connection

1:33 — Ask before offering a solution
Perhaps Leila thinks a fixed weekly slot is the only acceptable contribution. Perhaps she worries that everyone else knows more about gardening. Perhaps she does not know who will greet her. An occasional commitment, a clear first task, or a named person to meet might help with different problems. She must also be free to say the activity no longer fits.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#does-the-activity-offer-choice-confidence-and-connection

1:56 — Look at the arrangements
The garden’s rules matter too. Saturday mornings may suit existing members while excluding people with other commitments. A rota records garden attendance, not all the care Leila gives her father. The organiser can ask what makes taking part difficult without demanding private details as proof of commitment. Both sides still need a workable agreement and enough people to run the activity safely.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#who-gets-a-workable-opportunity

2:22 — Make the next step possible
Once Leila chooses a feasible arrangement, she can make an if–then plan. If the care visit finishes by ten, I will take the ten-twenty bus and meet Sam at the gate. If it runs late, I will message Sam and use the next agreed date. The plan connects a situation to an action. It cannot solve having no available transport.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#make-the-next-step-specificafter-it-becomes-feasible

2:45 — One visit is one result
If Leila attends a shorter session, that shows it was possible on that occasion. It does not prove the only obstacle was transport or that she now has a habit. If she still cannot come, ask what happened before increasing pressure. A different commitment, or a decision not to reserve a regular place, may suit everyone better.
Chapter explanation: https://snowch.github.io/understanding-people/chapters/03-needs-motives-goals.html#review-what-the-change-actually-tells-you

3:06 — One question to remember
When you or someone else wants to act but keeps missing it, ask: What is making it hard to do? Check interest, practical opportunity and the next step. Choose one difficulty that can be addressed, agree a change, and review what actually happened. Understanding motivation includes accepting that someone may choose to decline.
