Why we misinterpret people. A short refresher.

When you feel sure why someone acted, ask three questions. What did they say or do? Why do I think they did it? How could I check?

Here is a new fictional example. You offer to organise your father's appointment. He says, I'll do it myself. You think, He doesn't trust me.

You know he declined that offer. You are guessing why. He might want to choose the appointment time, prefer a different kind of help, or simply have it under control. These are possibilities, not a complete list.

You could ask: Would help with one part be useful, or would you prefer to handle it? Respect his answer. Later, compare it with an occasion when he accepted help. What differed?

Notice confirmation bias: are you remembering refusals and overlooking accepted help? Notice correspondence bias: are you deciding he is stubborn without considering this particular offer?

After trying something, review what happened. Did you understand his preference better? Did your response leave room for his choice? A respectful no can still be a useful outcome.
