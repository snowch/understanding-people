# Tendencies, learned expectations and consequences

8 November 2026. This is a generic extension of existing concepts, without assigning a role, motive, personality type or diagnosis to a real person.

- **Consequences that sustain a pattern** now compares a tendency across settings, an expectation learned from experience, and a consequence affecting later behaviour. It separates origins from current maintenance, and automatic habit from a conscious plan. The main comparison is a table; the practical exercise asks for an occasion, an exception, two possible explanations, counterevidence and one safe action to review.
- **Repeated criticism** applies that distinction without treating a critical style as a complete explanation. Silence does not establish agreement; different conduct with different people is information about context rather than proof of a calculated strategy.
- **People-pleasing** describes a possible request → anticipated reaction → agreement → immediate relief → later agreement cycle. The explanation distinguishes predicted, previously experienced and explicitly threatened reactions. It covers real constraints, freely chosen generosity, costs to other people and responsibility for coercion. Appeasement/conflict-avoidant accommodation is descriptive vocabulary, not a diagnosed trauma response.
- Navigation from Chapter 6, learned expectations, conflict avoidance and interaction cycles leads to these explanations. The unchanged foundations remain narrated. No extra reference pages or map nodes are created.

Selected existing sources support the background: Fleeson on variation across occasions; Wood and Rünger on habit; Hofmann and Hay on avoidance and its function; Frazier and colleagues on psychological safety; and Keltner and colleagues on power. These selected studies/reviews do not validate the proposed personal comparison, prove an individual motive, or make refusal safe. New examples, questions and response suggestions are editorial applications.
