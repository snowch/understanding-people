# Book content development plan

Agreed direction, 2 November 2026.

Develop **a concise book supported by a substantial reference library**, with the app helping readers apply what they learn. Depth should come from better explanations and useful comparisons, not longer stories. The purpose is to help readers understand themselves and others and collaborate more positively.

At this planning baseline, the repository contains twelve draft chapters and three dedicated deeper references: confirmation bias, correspondence bias and declining help. Chapter 1 follows the newer concept-first approach; much of the remaining book still follows extended fictional scenarios.

Implementation update: the [reference inventory](reference-inventory.md) now maps all 111 app reference topics to book URLs, development status and foundation chapters. The three developed entries remain; 108 additional entries are explicitly labelled stubs. These outlines establish coverage and navigation, not completion of the research or writing workflow below. Practice goals and skills remain separate.

## Map the content before writing more

Create a content inventory connecting each topic to:

- The reader's question.
- Its foundation chapter and reference entry.
- Relevant app nodes and practice journeys.
- Supporting research, limitations and missing evidence.
- Its status: outline, drafted, checked or published.

Several app nodes may belong in one reference entry. A graph node should not automatically generate another page.

## Give the book and library distinct jobs

| Layer | What it should provide |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation chapters | The concepts needed to understand people and make better decisions, in a readable sequence. |
| Reference library | Detailed explanations readers can find through a behaviour, question or technical term. |
| Practice | One compact exercise per chapter, with optional deeper work and an app route. |
| Audio | Optional explanations and listening exercises based on stable, checked text. |

Keep the twelve-chapter structure initially. Rewrite Chapters 2–12 around concepts, using short examples where they clarify something. Move specialist detail into references rather than continually expanding chapters.

Keep the existing topic-based reference navigation. Entries and recordings belong under their topics, not in an ever-growing list of top-level pages.

## Build depth across the required areas

| Area | Content needed |
| --- | --- |
| Interpreting behaviour | Observation versus interpretation; confirmation and correspondence biases; motivated reasoning; memory; uncertainty; competing explanations. |
| Needs, intentions and decisions | Autonomy, competence, belonging, competing goals, incentives, perceived costs, avoidance and the difference between intention and impact. |
| Personality and development | Big Five dimensions and useful facets; traits versus temporary states; temperament; stability and change; family resemblance, genetics and learned patterns. |
| Emotion, learning and circumstances | Stress, shame, anger, coping, habits, reinforcement, resources, accessibility and changes in health or functioning. |
| Relationships and social context | Trust, reciprocity, family expectations, culture, status, power, dependence, group norms and communication differences. |
| Recognisable behaviours | Declining help, criticism, defensiveness, interruption, withdrawal, reassurance-seeking, broken commitments, unwanted advice and positive behaviours such as repair and consideration. |
| Harmful patterns and clinical vocabulary | Gaslighting, coercive control, threats and manipulation; distinctions between traits, everyday labels and clinical terms; when support matters more than further interpretation. |
| Responding and collaborating | Listening, requests, feedback, disagreement, boundaries, negotiation, encouragement, repair and reviewing whether an approach helped. |

Healthy interaction needs as much attention as difficult behaviour. Otherwise the library will train readers to look mainly for problems. These areas define the coverage to develop; they are not an exhaustive classification of human behaviour.

## Use a consistent reference entry structure

Each substantial entry should answer:

1. **What does this mean?** A plain-English definition and short example.
2. **How might it arise?** Supported mechanisms, clearly separated from plausible explanations.
3. **What else could explain it?** Alternatives and commonly confused concepts.
4. **How would I investigate it?** Patterns, circumstances, exceptions and information that distinguishes explanations.
5. **How might I contribute?** My assumptions, actions and part in the interaction.
6. **What could help?** Options, trade-offs and conditions under which each makes sense.
7. **What would change my understanding?** Evidence that weakens the first explanation.
8. **Where next?** Related concepts, sources, optional practice and the corresponding app journey.

For example, a family pattern of declining help should lead readers through independence, preferences, past experiences, obligations, family learning and possible inherited tendencies, while explaining why resemblance alone cannot tell us their relative contributions.

## Establish a research and editorial workflow

For each topic:

1. Define the claims before drafting.
2. Find suitable reviews and foundational studies; check newer and conflicting evidence.
3. Record what each source actually supports, including population and setting.
4. Write the explanation in everyday English.
5. Add contrasting examples across different settings.
6. Perform a separate editorial pass to check source support, clarity and practical advice. Do not describe editorial or automated checks as specialist review.
7. Check the glossary, app connections and existing recordings for consistency.

Specialist review is not planned and is not a publication requirement. Clinical and abuse-related entries should include this standing caveat:

> This material provides general information and has not received specialist review. It is not a diagnostic tool or an individual assessment of safety. For advice about your circumstances, seek appropriately qualified support.

Careful sourcing and editorial checks remain part of the workflow. The caveat makes the limits clear; it does not establish accuracy. Automated checks can verify links and structure, but they cannot establish scientific accuracy.

Use one canonical source for shared book/app content, extending the existing reference-sync approach. Follow the [writing conventions in the README](../README.md#writing) and the [evidence standards](../evidence.md). Preserve existing URLs, heading anchors and practice-note identifiers when revising content.

## Deliver in manageable releases

| Order | Deliverable | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Inventory and editorial specification | Coverage matrix, entry template, evidence checklist and prioritised backlog. |
| 2 | Depth benchmark | Strengthen the three existing references, then develop personality/family resemblance, stress, listening and cooperation entries. Demonstrate breadth beyond interpreting ambiguous behaviour. |
| 3 | Foundation revision | Revise Chapters 2–12, linking to deeper material and retaining compact practice. |
| 4 | First substantial reference edition | Approximately 25 well-developed entries, selected for usefulness and coverage. This is a planning target, not a requirement to create pages for their own sake. |
| 5 | Expansion and audio | Add further entries from real lookup gaps; produce recordings once their text is stable. Review existing media for mismatches. |

## Judge content by what readers can do

After reading, can the reader explain the concept, distinguish it from alternatives, notice something useful in a fresh situation, and choose a constructive next step?

Remembering the terminology alone is not enough. Practice should test application to a fresh example, then support an observation–action–review journey in everyday life. Review whether it helped the reader understand and collaborate, including when a useful outcome is a respectful disagreement, a boundary or seeking support.
