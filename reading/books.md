# Books for deeper reading

Several books explore parts of this course in greater depth. This guide helps you choose one for the question you have now. It compares their focus and reading style; it is not a full critical review of every edition or an endorsement of every claim.

## Where to start {#start}

- **For recurring relationship patterns:** start with [The Dance of Anger](#dance-of-anger), then [Difficult Conversations](#difficult-conversations).
- **For a broad scientific foundation:** start with [The Social Animal](#social-animal). Choose [The Personality Puzzle](#personality-puzzle) for individual differences.
- **For biology, development and culture together:** choose [Behave](#behave) for a substantial explanation across different timescales.
- **For why a behaviour persists:** choose [Learning and Behavior](#learning-and-behavior), especially if you want more detail on learning, consequences and context.

You can read selectively. One useful idea that changes what you notice is a better starting point than trying to finish all eight books.

## The Social Animal {#social-animal}

**Elliot Aronson and Joshua Aronson · Broad social psychology**

A starting point for how other people and social situations affect judgment and behaviour. It connects topics such as conformity, influence and self-justification. Use it to broaden an explanation beyond someone's character. It is a foundation in social psychology; the course also draws on other disciplines.

Read alongside [Chapter 1: Why we misinterpret people](../chapters/01-before-you-explain.md) and [Chapter 7: Culture, groups and power](../chapters/07-culture-groups-power.md). [Publisher details: twelfth edition](https://www.macmillanlearning.com/college/us/product/The-Social-Animal/p/1464144184). Earlier editions have different authorship credits.

## Behave: The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst {#behave}

**Robert M. Sapolsky · Substantial popular science**

Sapolsky connects brain processes and hormones with development, learning, culture and evolution. He examines influences across different timescales, from the circumstances immediately before an action to a lifetime of experience and longer histories. It is particularly useful for resisting a single-cause explanation of behaviour. Expect a dense scientific overview rather than a step-by-step guide to handling a particular interaction.

Read alongside [Chapter 5: Emotion, stress and coping](../chapters/05-emotion-stress-coping.md), [Chapter 6: Personality, habits and situations](../chapters/06-personality-habits-situations.md) and [Chapter 7: Culture, groups and power](../chapters/07-culture-groups-power.md). [UK publisher details](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/414929/behave-by-robert-sapolsky/9780099575061).

## The Personality Puzzle {#personality-puzzle}

**David C. Funder · Substantial textbook**

For a deeper account of personality, how it is studied and how accurately people judge one another. Useful when you want to compare a broad tendency with variation across situations. Expect more theory and research methods than in a short practical guide. A trait description still needs context when explaining a particular action.

Read alongside [Chapter 6: Personality, habits and situations](../chapters/06-personality-habits-situations.md). [Publisher details](https://wwnorton.com/books/The-Personality-Puzzle/).

## Learning and Behavior: A Contemporary Synthesis {#learning-and-behavior}

**Mark E. Bouton · Technical learning textbook**

For the science of learning and conditioning, including how experience and context affect behaviour. Particularly relevant to distinguishing a general tendency, a learned expectation and the consequences sustaining a pattern. It takes more study than a relationship guide. Applying a learning principle to everyday life requires checking what actually happens; it does not establish an individual's motive.

Read alongside [Consequences that sustain a pattern](../reference/reinforcement.md#tendency-expectation-consequence) and [Learned expectations](../reference/learned.md). [Library catalogue record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8562675W).

## Mistakes Were Made (But Not by Me) {#mistakes-were-made}

**Carol Tavris and Elliot Aronson · Accessible psychology**

For self-justification: how people defend earlier choices and their view of themselves, and why acknowledging an error can be difficult. This complements our treatment of confirmation bias, although the concepts are not identical. Bring the explanation back to your own reasoning as well as someone else's.

Read alongside [Confirmation bias](../reference/confirmation-bias.md). [Library catalogue record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15014628W).

## The Dance of Anger {#dance-of-anger}

**Harriet Lerner · Practical relationship guide**

Lerner's guide focuses on women and patterns in intimate relationships. It is relevant to anger, accommodation, family expectations and changing your own participation in a recurring exchange. Its clinical perspective offers practical ideas; it does not establish a family member's personality or make a response safe in every relationship. Understanding a cycle does not imply equal responsibility for harm.

Read alongside [People-pleasing and accommodation](../reference/people-pleasing.md#agreement-relief-cycle) and [Family membership, roles and loyalties](../reference/family-membership.md). [Library catalogue record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL271818W).

## Difficult Conversations {#difficult-conversations}

**Douglas Stone, Bruce Patton and Sheila Heen · Practical communication guide**

For conversations involving different accounts of what happened, intentions, impact, feelings and identity. Useful when you want to raise a concern and understand another perspective without making agreement the only acceptable outcome. A conversation framework cannot remove a serious power imbalance or guarantee a cooperative response.

Read alongside [Chapter 8: Questions, listening and disagreement](../chapters/08-questions-listening-disagreement.md) and [Listening and checking understanding](../reference/listening.md). [Library catalogue record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL13513526W).

## Influence {#influence}

**Robert B. Cialdini · Psychology of persuasion**

For understanding pressures surrounding agreement, including reciprocity, authority and other people's choices. Consider your own requests as well as influence directed at you. A persuasion principle is a possibility to examine, not a reliable way to secure compliance or evidence of a hidden strategy.

Read alongside [Chapter 10: Motivation, influence and cooperation](../chapters/10-motivation-influence-cooperation.md) and [Escalating requests and everyday influence](../reference/sequential-requests.md). [Author's book descriptions](https://www.influenceatwork.com/books-and-publications/).

## Use a book with the course {#use-with-course}

Choose one question, then use the course chapter or reference entry to find the relevant concept. Read the matching book for depth. Record an observation, a possible explanation, an action within your control and what happened afterward in the [situation worksheet](../worksheet.md). Keep exceptions and uncertainty visible.

This course connects established ideas with a searchable reference library and reusable practice. The books offer further depth; neither a book nor this course can settle why a particular person acted as they did from a short account.

This starter list emphasises psychology, relationships and conversation. It is not a complete reading list for sociology, anthropology, mental health or abuse. The [research bibliography](../references.md) and [reference-library sources](../reference/sources.md) show the selected evidence used for the course's claims.

## About these recommendations {#about-recommendations}

Titles, authorship and scope were checked against the linked publisher pages, author descriptions and library catalogue records on 10 November 2026. These coverage notes are original editorial recommendations, rather than a chapter-by-chapter assessment of scientific accuracy. Editions differ, so check the contents and format before choosing a copy.
