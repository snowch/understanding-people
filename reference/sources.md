# Research sources for the reference library

Selected research records, abstracts, accessible source sections and public guidance checked on 2 and 8 November 2026. Access scope and date are recorded per source; this is not a systematic review. Source wording, questionnaires, diagnostic checklists, figures and recordings are not reproduced. Explanations and fictional examples are original editorial writing.

Read sources beside the claims in each entry. Research findings, public guidance and editorial applications have different roles. Checking a link or the structure of a page does not establish scientific accuracy.

No third-party figures, questionnaire items, diagnostic checklists or recordings are reproduced. The source titles identify the cited works; explanations and fictional examples are original writing.

## Shukla and colleagues (2025). When and how do parent-child acculturation gaps matter? {#acculturation}

[Shukla and colleagues (2025). When and how do parent-child acculturation gaps matter?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40073496/)

Serena Shukla and colleagues reviewed 98 records concerning immigrant parents and children. More than half found no association between cultural gaps and the examined child, parenting or family outcomes.

Different rates of cultural adaptation are not inherently a family problem. Specific domains and circumstances matter.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40073496/).

## Brown & Gilman (1960). The Pronouns of Power and Solidarity. {#address}

[Brown & Gilman (1960). The Pronouns of Power and Solidarity.](https://pages.mtu.edu/~rlstrick/rsvtxt/pronoun.htm)

Roger Brown and Albert Gilman examined how address pronouns express power and solidarity, drawing on historical material and language informants.

The mainly European-language material does not show that a particular family uses names deliberately to establish rank.

**Access scope:** Accessible institutional text: introduction and power/solidarity discussion read. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pages.mtu.edu/~rlstrick/rsvtxt/pronoun.htm).

## Hu RX, Luo M, Zhang A, Li LW. (2021). Associations of Ageism and Health: A Systematic Review of Quantitative Observational Studies. {#ageism}

[Hu RX, Luo M, Zhang A, Li LW. (2021). Associations of Ageism and Health: A Systematic Review of Quantitative Observational Studies.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33317402/)

Hu and colleagues reviewed quantitative observational studies of ageism and health. Age-related stereotypes, discrimination and self-perceptions were associated with several health outcomes.

The studies were observational and measures varied; the review does not establish the cause of a particular person’s difficulty.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33317402).

## Sharma and colleagues. Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models (ICLR 2024; arXiv revision 2025). {#ai-agreement}

[Sharma and colleagues. Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models (ICLR 2024; arXiv revision 2025).](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)

Mrinank Sharma and colleagues evaluated five AI assistants and found that matching a user’s views could compete with giving accurate answers.

The evaluated models do not represent every current service. A prompt asking for alternatives is not a validated guarantee against sycophancy.

**Access scope:** Author abstract and version record read; not a claim of evaluating the app’s current providers. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548).

## Lareau (2002). Invisible Inequality: Social Class and Childrearing in Black Families and White Families. {#anthropology}

[Lareau (2002). Invisible Inequality: Social Class and Childrearing in Black Families and White Families.](https://doi.org/10.1177/000312240206700507)

Sociologist Annette Lareau used ethnographic observation and interviews with US families to examine how class-related practices affected interactions with institutions.

The study concerns particular families and settings; it is not a universal parenting typology or an explanation of an individual family.

**Access scope:** Publisher-supplied abstract and bibliographic record read through Crossref. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/000312240206700507).

## Lewicki, Polin & Lount (2016). An Exploration of the Structure of Effective Apologies. {#apology}

[Lewicki, Polin & Lount (2016). An Exploration of the Structure of Effective Apologies.](https://doi.org/10.1111/ncmr.12073)

Roy Lewicki and colleagues ran two studies of how people judged apologies after hypothetical trust violations. Apologies differed in perceived effectiveness depending on what they contained and the kind of violation.

Judging a scenario differs from rebuilding trust after real harm. No apology formula obliges forgiveness or renewed access.

**Access scope:** Publisher-supplied abstract and bibliographic record read through Crossref. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/ncmr.12073).

## Zhang and colleagues (2022). The relationship between adult attachment and mental health: A meta-analysis. {#attachment}

[Zhang and colleagues (2022). The relationship between adult attachment and mental health: A meta-analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36201836/)

Zhang and colleagues combined studies of attachment anxiety and avoidance. Both dimensions were associated with mental-health measures.

The associations do not diagnose anyone or establish a fixed attachment type or childhood cause.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36201836/).

## Hadden, Smith & Webster (2014). Relationship duration moderates associations between attachment and relationship quality. {#attachment-time}

[Hadden, Smith & Webster (2014). Relationship duration moderates associations between attachment and relationship quality.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24026179/)

Benjamin Hadden and colleagues reviewed associations between attachment dimensions, relationship satisfaction and commitment. Relationship duration moderated associations in cross-sectional samples.

Differences between longer and shorter relationships do not establish a person’s future or show that duration caused deterioration.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24026179/).

## Stevanovic & Peräkylä (2012). Deontic Authority in Interaction. {#authority}

[Stevanovic & Peräkylä (2012). Deontic Authority in Interaction.](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/deontic-authority-in-interaction-the-right-to-announce-propose-an/)

Melisa Stevanovic and Anssi Peräkylä analysed recorded workplace planning discussions. People could accept a plan while resisting another speaker’s assumed right to decide it.

Conversation analysis of a specific workplace does not establish every family’s decision rights or validate a tactic detector.

**Access scope:** Institutional research record and abstract read. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/deontic-authority-in-interaction-the-right-to-announce-propose-an/).

## Crompton and colleagues (2020). Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective. {#autistic-communication}

[Crompton and colleagues (2020). Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32431157/)

Catherine Crompton and colleagues studied story-information transfer in autistic, non-autistic and mixed groups. Transfer and rapport were poorer in mixed groups than within same-neurotype groups in this task.

This supports attention to communication fit, not a rule about every autistic person or all forms of neurodivergence.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32431157).

## Hofmann & Hay (2018). Rethinking avoidance: Toward a balanced approach to avoidance in treating anxiety disorders. {#avoidance-review}

[Hofmann & Hay (2018). Rethinking avoidance: Toward a balanced approach to avoidance in treating anxiety disorders.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550689/)

Stefan Hofmann and Angela Hay reviewed avoidance in anxiety treatment, including circumstances in which avoidance can be adaptive.

Function and actual danger matter. This is not a reason to remove someone’s safety arrangements or conduct exposure treatment yourself.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29550689).

## Baumeister & Leary (1995). The need to belong: desire for interpersonal attachments as a fundamental human motivation. {#belonging}

[Baumeister & Leary (1995). The need to belong: desire for interpersonal attachments as a fundamental human motivation.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7777651/)

Roy Baumeister and Mark Leary reviewed evidence for a widespread motivation to form and maintain stable interpersonal attachments.

The review does not imply that everyone wants the same amount or form of contact, or must maintain an unsafe relationship.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7777651).

## Soto & John (2017). The next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and assessing a hierarchical model with 15 facets to enhance bandwidth, fidelity, and predictive power. {#bigfive}

[Soto & John (2017). The next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and assessing a hierarchical model with 15 facets to enhance bandwidth, fidelity, and predictive power.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27055049/)

Christopher Soto and Oliver John developed and tested the BFI-2 across three studies, organising personality descriptions into five broad dimensions and narrower facets.

These are dimensions, not fixed types or clinical diagnoses. This entry does not reproduce, administer or score the questionnaire.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27055049).

## CDC: Developmental milestones {#cdc-development}

[CDC: Developmental milestones](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/index.html)

CDC public guidance describes development in play, learning, communication, action and movement, and recommends discussing concerns or lost skills with a clinician.

Its milestone resources are not substitutes for validated screening and do not diagnose a child from one missed task.

## Michie, van Stralen & West (2011). The behaviour change wheel: a new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions. {#comb}

[Michie, van Stralen & West (2011). The behaviour change wheel: a new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21513547/)

Susan Michie and colleagues reviewed behaviour-change frameworks and proposed COM-B: capability, opportunity and motivation as interacting conditions for behaviour. They examined its use in tobacco-control and obesity policy.

It is a framework for analysing and designing change, not a diagnostic test or proof that one identified barrier caused an action.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21513547).

## Social comparison and envy on social media: A critical review (2022). {#comparison}

[Social comparison and envy on social media: A critical review (2022).](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35184028/)

A 2022 critical review examined social comparison, envy and well-being on social media. The evidence included mixed, conditional and person-dependent findings.

Comparison is not uniformly harmful, and findings about social media cannot explain every offline comparison.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35184028/).

## Vignoles and colleagues (2016). Beyond the 'east-west' dichotomy: Global variation in cultural models of selfhood. {#culture}

[Vignoles and colleagues (2016). Beyond the 'east-west' dichotomy: Global variation in cultural models of selfhood.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27359126/)

Vivian Vignoles and colleagues conducted two multinational surveys. Ways of being independent and connected varied across several dimensions, beyond a simple East–West division.

Group patterns cannot reveal one person’s values, and national averages are not instructions for interacting with an individual.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27359126).

## Ross and colleagues (2019). Communication that is maladaptive for middle-class couples is adaptive for socioeconomically disadvantaged couples. {#cycle-context}

[Ross and colleagues (2019). Communication that is maladaptive for middle-class couples is adaptive for socioeconomically disadvantaged couples.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30321045/)

Jaclyn Ross and colleagues followed couples over time. Associations between demand–withdraw communication and relationship satisfaction varied with socioeconomic resources.

These observational studies do not prescribe withdrawal or excuse coercion; practical capacity can change what a request and response mean.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30321045/).

## Sezer and colleagues (2025). An experimental investigation of daily mental contrasting with implementation intentions and goal motives in reducing bedtime procrastination. {#daily-plans}

[Sezer and colleagues (2025). An experimental investigation of daily mental contrasting with implementation intentions and goal motives in reducing bedtime procrastination.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40219795/)

Sezer and colleagues compared daily with one-off planning in a week-long registered study of 297 participants. The daily group reported less bedtime procrastination.

The specific comparison and short duration do not establish a durable treatment for every kind of procrastination.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40219795/).

## Harsey & Freyd (2023). The Influence of Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender and Insincere Apologies on Perceptions of Sexual Assault. {#darvo}

[Harsey & Freyd (2023). The Influence of Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender and Insincere Apologies on Perceptions of Sexual Assault.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37154429/)

Sarah Harsey and Jennifer Freyd experimentally varied fictional sexual-assault accounts read by 230 undergraduates. Denial, attack and blame reversal affected judgments of the people described.

This concerns observers’ judgments in a specific scenario. It is not a test of guilt, family prevalence or personality disorder.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37154429/).

## Bond & DePaulo (2006). Accuracy of deception judgments. {#deception}

[Bond & DePaulo (2006). Accuracy of deception judgments.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/)

Charles Bond and Bella DePaulo combined studies of unaided truth–lie judgments. Average accuracy was about 54%, with truths recognised more accurately than lies.

This is an average across tasks, not the probability that a particular person is lying.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16859438).

## Schrodt, Witt & Shimkowski (2014). A Meta-Analytical Review of the Demand/Withdraw Pattern of Interaction. {#demand-withdraw}

[Schrodt, Witt & Shimkowski (2014). A Meta-Analytical Review of the Demand/Withdraw Pattern of Interaction.](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637751.2013.813632)

Paul Schrodt and colleagues combined 74 studies linking demand–withdraw interaction with relational, communication and individual outcomes. The sequence concerns one person pressing for change while another disengages.

The associations do not establish who caused a conflict or equal responsibility for harm.

**Access scope:** Abstract read through EBSCO bibliographic record; publisher full text restricted. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637751.2013.813632).

## Jensen & Thomsen (2024). Parental differential treatment of siblings linked with internalizing and externalizing behavior: A meta-analysis. {#differential}

[Jensen & Thomsen (2024). Parental differential treatment of siblings linked with internalizing and externalizing behavior: A meta-analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38439142/)

Jensen and Thomsen combined research involving 26,451 participants. Differences in parental treatment were associated with children’s internalising and externalising difficulties.

The largely White child/adolescent samples do not establish an adult in-law’s motive, and association does not identify a single cause.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38439142/).

## Kruger, Epley, Parker & Ng (2005). Egocentrism over e-mail: can we communicate as well as we think? {#digital-tone}

[Kruger, Epley, Parker & Ng (2005). Egocentrism over e-mail: can we communicate as well as we think?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16393025/)

Justin Kruger and colleagues ran five experiments suggesting that people overestimate how well an intended tone comes across in email.

The experiments do not show that every short message is misunderstood or that a delayed reply is harmless. Contemporary platforms and relationships may differ.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16393025/).

## Cropanzano & Mitchell (2005). Social Exchange Theory: An Interdisciplinary Review. {#exchange}

[Cropanzano & Mitchell (2005). Social Exchange Theory: An Interdisciplinary Review.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206305279602)

Russell Cropanzano and Marie Mitchell reviewed social-exchange theory in organisational research, including different exchange rules, resources and relationships.

## Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018). Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective. {#eyal}

[Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018). Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/)

Tal Eyal and colleagues tested interpersonal predictions across a series of experiments. Imagining another person’s perspective did not consistently improve accuracy; a final experiment found that obtaining information through conversation helped.

Conversation does not guarantee honesty, complete understanding or safety. These were specific prediction tasks, not tests of every relationship.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29620401).

## Stewart and colleagues. The finer details? The predictability of life outcomes from Big Five domains, facets, and nuances. {#facets}

[Stewart and colleagues. The finer details? The predictability of life outcomes from Big Five domains, facets, and nuances.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34236710/)

Ross Stewart and colleagues compared broad traits, facets and narrower descriptions when predicting 53 self-reported outcomes in a large dataset with separate training and validation samples. Narrower descriptions carried useful information.

Prediction is not a causal account. The study does not license scoring someone from selected anecdotes or copying assessment items.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34236710/).

## Cardon & Okoro (2010). A Meta-Analysis of the Cultural Propositions about Conflict Management Styles in Face-Negotiation Theory. {#facework}

[Cardon & Okoro (2010). A Meta-Analysis of the Cultural Propositions about Conflict Management Styles in Face-Negotiation Theory.](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/rpcg/vol1/iss1/2/)

Peter Cardon and Ephraim Okoro reviewed studies testing cultural propositions about conflict styles and face: the respect or social identity people seek to maintain in interaction.

**Access scope:** Institutional abstract and bibliographic record only; detailed results not reviewed. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/rpcg/vol1/iss1/2/).

## Colquitt and colleagues (2001). Justice at the millennium: a meta-analytic review of 25 years of organizational justice research. {#fairness}

[Colquitt and colleagues (2001). Justice at the millennium: a meta-analytic review of 25 years of organizational justice research.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419803/)

Jason Colquitt and colleagues combined 183 organisational-justice studies. Fair outcomes, procedures, interpersonal treatment and information were related but distinguishable aspects of perceived fairness.

Most evidence concerns organisations. Disagreement about fairness is not proof of dishonesty or a single objectively correct allocation.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11419803).

## Carroll, Olson & Buckmiller (2007). Family Boundary Ambiguity: A 30-Year Review. {#family-boundary}

[Carroll, Olson & Buckmiller (2007). Family Boundary Ambiguity: A 30-Year Review.](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/4337/)

Jason Carroll and colleagues reviewed 37 studies across settings including divorce, stepfamilies, illness and caregiving. Boundary ambiguity concerns uncertainty about family membership or roles.

This differs from deciding a personal limit. The review does not supply a checklist for assigning family roles or blame.

**Access scope:** Institutional abstract and research record read. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/4337/).

## Cook, Dezangré & De Mol (2018). Sources of perceived responsiveness in family relationships. {#family-response}

[Cook, Dezangré & De Mol (2018). Sources of perceived responsiveness in family relationships.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29745679/)

William Cook and colleagues studied 207 two-parent, two-child families. Perceived responsiveness reflected contributions from the perceiver, the other person and their particular relationship.

The design does not establish a causal remedy or represent all family structures. One interaction is not simply a personality reading.

**Access scope:** NCBI bibliographic record and abstract read; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29745679/).

## Kluger & DeNisi (1996). The effects of feedback interventions on performance. {#feedback-process}

[Kluger & DeNisi (1996). The effects of feedback interventions on performance.](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-interventions-on-performance-a-historical/)

Avraham Kluger and Angelo DeNisi combined feedback experiments. Performance improved on average, but more than a third of the interventions reduced it. Their account emphasises where feedback directs attention.

No fixed wording or praise formula follows from the average result; task and context matter.

**Access scope:** Institutional abstract and research record read. Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-interventions-on-performance-a-historical/).

## Bonanno & Burton (2013). Regulatory Flexibility: An Individual Differences Perspective on Coping and Emotion Regulation. {#flexibility}

Psychologists Daniel Gilbert and Patrick Malone reviewed research on correspondence bias: people can underweight circumstances when explaining another person’s behaviour.

## Grayson, Feinberg, Willer & Zaki (2025). Ironic effects of prosocial gossip in driving inaccurate social perceptions. {#gossip-evidence}

[Grayson, Feinberg, Willer & Zaki (2025). Ironic effects of prosocial gossip in driving inaccurate social perceptions.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2024.104682)

Samantha Grayson and colleagues reanalysed economic-game gossip and ran an experiment with new readers. Negative stories were disproportionately shared, leading readers to underestimate how cooperatively the original groups behaved.

Economic-game findings do not establish that every warning is biased or that a specific family’s reputation is false.

**Access scope:** Abstract read in Stanford GSB Preserve institutional record: https://gsbpreserve.stanford.edu/view/43150/ironic-effects-of-prosocial-gossip-in-driving-inaccurate-social-perceptions Checked 2026-11-08. [Source record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2024.104682).

[Wood & Rünger (2016). Psychology of Habit.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/)

Wendy Wood and Dennis Rünger reviewed habit research. Repetition in recurring contexts can make responses more automatic, alongside deliberate goals.

## Polderman and colleagues (2015). Meta-analysis of the heritability of human traits based on fifty years of twin studies. {#heritability}

Tinca Polderman and colleagues combined twin-study findings across many human traits. Genetic contributions to variation were widespread across the studied traits.

Heritability concerns variation in studied populations, not the genetic percentage of one action. Family resemblance does not separate inheritance from learning.
