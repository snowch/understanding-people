# Research sources for the reference library

Selected research records/abstracts and public guidance checked on 2 November 2026. Access scope is recorded per source; this is not a systematic review. Source wording, questionnaires, diagnostic checklists, figures and recordings are not reproduced. All explanatory prose and teaching examples are original editorial writing.

Read sources beside the claims in each entry. Research findings, public guidance and editorial applications have different roles. Checking a link or the structure of a page does not establish scientific accuracy.

No third-party figures, questionnaire items, diagnostic checklists or recordings are reproduced. The source titles identify the cited works; explanations and fictional examples are original writing.

## Hu RX, Luo M, Zhang A, Li LW. (2021). Associations of Ageism and Health: A Systematic Review of Quantitative Observational Studies. {#ageism}

[Hu RX, Luo M, Zhang A, Li LW. (2021). Associations of Ageism and Health: A Systematic Review of Quantitative Observational Studies.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33317402/)

Hu and colleagues reviewed quantitative observational studies of ageism and health. Age-related stereotypes, discrimination and self-perceptions were associated with several health outcomes.

The studies were observational and measures varied; the review does not establish the cause of a particular person’s difficulty.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33317402).

## Lareau (2002). Invisible Inequality: Social Class and Childrearing in Black Families and White Families. {#anthropology}

[Lareau (2002). Invisible Inequality: Social Class and Childrearing in Black Families and White Families.](https://doi.org/10.1177/000312240206700507)

Sociologist Annette Lareau used ethnographic observation and interviews with US families to examine how class-related practices affected interactions with institutions.

The study concerns particular families and settings; it is not a universal parenting typology or an explanation of an individual family.

**Access scope:** Publisher-supplied abstract and bibliographic record read through Crossref. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/000312240206700507).

## Lewicki, Polin & Lount (2016). An Exploration of the Structure of Effective Apologies. {#apology}

[Lewicki, Polin & Lount (2016). An Exploration of the Structure of Effective Apologies.](https://doi.org/10.1111/ncmr.12073)

Roy Lewicki and colleagues ran two studies of how people judged apologies after hypothetical trust violations. Apologies differed in perceived effectiveness depending on what they contained and the kind of violation.

Judging a scenario differs from rebuilding trust after real harm. No apology formula obliges forgiveness or renewed access.

**Access scope:** Publisher-supplied abstract and bibliographic record read through Crossref. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/ncmr.12073).

## Crompton and colleagues (2020). Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective. {#autistic-communication}

[Crompton and colleagues (2020). Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32431157/)

Catherine Crompton and colleagues studied story-information transfer in autistic, non-autistic and mixed groups. Transfer and rapport were poorer in mixed groups than within same-neurotype groups in this task.

This supports attention to communication fit, not a rule about every autistic person or all forms of neurodivergence.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32431157).

## Hofmann & Hay (2018). Rethinking avoidance: Toward a balanced approach to avoidance in treating anxiety disorders. {#avoidance-review}

[Hofmann & Hay (2018). Rethinking avoidance: Toward a balanced approach to avoidance in treating anxiety disorders.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550689/)

Stefan Hofmann and Angela Hay reviewed avoidance in anxiety treatment, including circumstances in which avoidance can be adaptive.

Function and actual danger matter. This is not a reason to remove someone’s safety arrangements or conduct exposure treatment yourself.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29550689).

## Baumeister & Leary (1995). The need to belong: desire for interpersonal attachments as a fundamental human motivation. {#belonging}

[Baumeister & Leary (1995). The need to belong: desire for interpersonal attachments as a fundamental human motivation.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7777651/)

Roy Baumeister and Mark Leary reviewed evidence for a widespread motivation to form and maintain stable interpersonal attachments.

The review does not imply that everyone wants the same amount or form of contact, or must maintain an unsafe relationship.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7777651).

## Soto & John (2017). The next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and assessing a hierarchical model with 15 facets to enhance bandwidth, fidelity, and predictive power. {#bigfive}

[Soto & John (2017). The next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and assessing a hierarchical model with 15 facets to enhance bandwidth, fidelity, and predictive power.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27055049/)

Christopher Soto and Oliver John developed and tested the BFI-2 across three studies, organising personality descriptions into five broad dimensions and narrower facets.

These are dimensions, not fixed types or clinical diagnoses. This entry does not reproduce, administer or score the questionnaire.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27055049).

## Michie, van Stralen & West (2011). The behaviour change wheel: a new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions. {#comb}

[Michie, van Stralen & West (2011). The behaviour change wheel: a new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21513547/)

Susan Michie and colleagues reviewed behaviour-change frameworks and proposed COM-B: capability, opportunity and motivation as interacting conditions for behaviour. They examined its use in tobacco-control and obesity policy.

It is a framework for analysing and designing change, not a diagnostic test or proof that one identified barrier caused an action.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21513547).

## Vignoles and colleagues (2016). Beyond the 'east-west' dichotomy: Global variation in cultural models of selfhood. {#culture}

[Vignoles and colleagues (2016). Beyond the 'east-west' dichotomy: Global variation in cultural models of selfhood.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27359126/)

Vivian Vignoles and colleagues conducted two multinational surveys. Ways of being independent and connected varied across several dimensions, beyond a simple East–West division.

Group patterns cannot reveal one person’s values, and national averages are not instructions for interacting with an individual.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27359126).

## Bond & DePaulo (2006). Accuracy of deception judgments. {#deception}

[Bond & DePaulo (2006). Accuracy of deception judgments.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/)

Charles Bond and Bella DePaulo combined studies of unaided truth–lie judgments. Average accuracy was about 54%, with truths recognised more accurately than lies.

This is an average across tasks, not the probability that a particular person is lying.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16859438).

## Cropanzano & Mitchell (2005). Social Exchange Theory: An Interdisciplinary Review. {#exchange}

[Cropanzano & Mitchell (2005). Social Exchange Theory: An Interdisciplinary Review.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206305279602)

Russell Cropanzano and Marie Mitchell reviewed social-exchange theory in organisational research, including different exchange rules, resources and relationships.

The review also identifies conceptual ambiguity. Relationships cannot be reduced to a universal ledger of favours.

**Access scope:** Publisher-supplied abstract and bibliographic record read through Crossref. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0149206305279602).

## Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018). Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective. {#eyal}

[Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018). Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/)

Tal Eyal and colleagues tested interpersonal predictions across a series of experiments. Imagining another person’s perspective did not consistently improve accuracy; a final experiment found that obtaining information through conversation helped.

Conversation does not guarantee honesty, complete understanding or safety. These were specific prediction tasks, not tests of every relationship.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29620401).

## Colquitt and colleagues (2001). Justice at the millennium: a meta-analytic review of 25 years of organizational justice research. {#fairness}

[Colquitt and colleagues (2001). Justice at the millennium: a meta-analytic review of 25 years of organizational justice research.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419803/)

Jason Colquitt and colleagues combined 183 organisational-justice studies. Fair outcomes, procedures, interpersonal treatment and information were related but distinguishable aspects of perceived fairness.

Most evidence concerns organisations. Disagreement about fairness is not proof of dishonesty or a single objectively correct allocation.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11419803).

## Bonanno & Burton (2013). Regulatory Flexibility: An Individual Differences Perspective on Coping and Emotion Regulation. {#flexibility}

[Bonanno & Burton (2013). Regulatory Flexibility: An Individual Differences Perspective on Coping and Emotion Regulation.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173226/)

George Bonanno and Charles Burton reviewed coping and emotion-regulation research. Their framework emphasises noticing context, having several response options and adjusting to feedback.

It is a framework with acknowledged measurement limits, not evidence that one response or this worksheet always works.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26173226).

## Sweet (2019). The Sociology of Gaslighting. {#gaslighting}

[Sweet (2019). The Sociology of Gaslighting.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122419874843)

Sociologist Paige Sweet used domestic-violence accounts to develop an explanation of gaslighting involving power and structural inequalities.

This is a qualitative sociological analysis, not a validated checklist for classifying any disagreement.

**Access scope:** Publisher-supplied abstract and bibliographic record read through Crossref. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0003122419874843).

## Gilbert & Malone (1995). The correspondence bias. {#gilbert}

[Gilbert & Malone (1995). The correspondence bias.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7870861/)

Psychologists Daniel Gilbert and Patrick Malone reviewed research on correspondence bias: people can underweight circumstances when explaining another person’s behaviour.

This does not establish that a particular judgment about someone’s character is mistaken.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7870861).

## Wood & Rünger (2016). Psychology of Habit. {#habit}

[Wood & Rünger (2016). Psychology of Habit.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/)

Wendy Wood and Dennis Rünger reviewed habit research. Repetition in recurring contexts can make responses more automatic, alongside deliberate goals.

Not every repeated action is a habit. A pattern may also reflect ongoing constraints, decisions or coercion.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26361052).

## Polderman and colleagues (2015). Meta-analysis of the heritability of human traits based on fifty years of twin studies. {#heritability}

[Polderman and colleagues (2015). Meta-analysis of the heritability of human traits based on fifty years of twin studies.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25985137/)

Tinca Polderman and colleagues combined twin-study findings across many human traits. Genetic contributions to variation were widespread across the studied traits.

Heritability concerns variation in studied populations, not the genetic percentage of one action. Family resemblance does not separate inheritance from learning.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25985137).

## Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford (2014). Intrinsic motivation and extrinsic incentives jointly predict performance: a 40-year meta-analysis. {#incentives}

[Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford (2014). Intrinsic motivation and extrinsic incentives jointly predict performance: a 40-year meta-analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24491020/)

Christopher Cerasoli and colleagues combined research on motivation, incentives and performance. Intrinsic motivation and incentives both mattered, with differences between quality and quantity.

Rewards are neither universally helpful nor universally harmful; performance findings cannot identify one person’s values.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24491020).

## Webb & Sheeran (2006). Does changing behavioral intentions engender behavior change? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence. {#intentions}

[Webb & Sheeran (2006). Does changing behavioral intentions engender behavior change? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16536643/)

Thomas Webb and Paschal Sheeran combined 47 experimental tests in which intentions changed. Changes in behaviour were generally smaller than changes in intention.

The result supports separating a promise from follow-through, not deciding why an individual failed to act.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16536643).

## Kunda (1990). The case for motivated reasoning. {#kunda}

[Kunda (1990). The case for motivated reasoning.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2270237/)

Psychologist Ziva Kunda reviewed research on motivated reasoning. Desired conclusions can shape which reasons people search for and find convincing.

Disagreement alone cannot establish a hidden motive, and a desired conclusion can still be correct.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/2270237).

## Itzchakov, Kluger & Castro (2017). I Am Aware of My Inconsistencies but Can Tolerate Them: The Effect of High Quality Listening on Speakers' Attitude Ambivalence. {#listening}

[Itzchakov, Kluger & Castro (2017). I Am Aware of My Inconsistencies but Can Tolerate Them: The Effect of High Quality Listening on Speakers' Attitude Ambivalence.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27856728/)

Guy Itzchakov and colleagues manipulated listening quality in four laboratory experiments. Attentive, nonjudgmental listening reduced speakers’ social anxiety and helped them recognise mixed attitudes in the studied tasks.

The experiments do not show that listening guarantees agreement or repairs a harmful relationship.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27856728).

## Loftus (2005). Planting misinformation in the human mind: a 30-year investigation of the malleability of memory. {#memory}

[Loftus (2005). Planting misinformation in the human mind: a 30-year investigation of the malleability of memory.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16027179/)

Elizabeth Loftus reviewed experimental research on misinformation after an event. Later suggestions can change what people report remembering.

This does not show that a particular memory is false, or justify dismissing an account of harm.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16027179).

## Du TV, Miller JD, Lynam DR. (2022). The relation between narcissism and aggression: A meta-analysis. {#narcissism-components}

[Du TV, Miller JD, Lynam DR. (2022). The relation between narcissism and aggression: A meta-analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34689345/)

Du, Miller and Lynam conducted a preregistered meta-analysis of narcissism components and aggression. Associations differed across components, showing why a single broad label can conceal meaningful differences.

Research components and measures are not a home classification system. Results cannot identify a clinical disorder or a hidden strategy from an incident log.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34689345).

## Kjærvik SL, Bushman BJ. (2021). The link between narcissism and aggression: A meta-analytic review. {#narcissism-meta}

[Kjærvik SL, Bushman BJ. (2021). The link between narcissism and aggression: A meta-analytic review.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34292012/)

Sophie Kjærvik and Brad Bushman combined 437 studies on narcissism and aggression. Research measures of narcissism were associated with aggression on average, with stronger links in provocation conditions.

These average associations do not diagnose a person, establish the cause of one action or predict individual violence. Do not provoke someone to test a trait.

**Access scope:** Research record and abstract read through Europe PMC; no claim of full-text review. Checked 2026-11-02. [Source record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34292012).

## NIMH: Anxiety disorders {#nimh-anxiety}
