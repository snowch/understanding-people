# Use AI without reinforcing a first impression

An AI assistant can help organise an account, explain a general concept or suggest questions. It does not observe your relationship. Its response is generated from the information and wording supplied, and may agree with your interpretation while sounding independent. A polished explanation is not new evidence about another person.

> **Remember:** Ask for alternatives; verify claims; keep the decision yours.

The brief example is fictional. Possible explanations and practical suggestions are editorial applications; they are not a test that identifies a motive, trait or diagnosis.

<span id="what-the-full-entry-will-cover"></span>

## How it may arise

A prompt such as “Why is my relative manipulating me?” assumes the conclusion before analysis begins. A model may build a coherent story around that wording, invent missing history or present general tendencies as an individual explanation. Supplying more incidents does not eliminate selection bias or missing perspectives.

## What the evidence supports

Mrinank Sharma and colleagues evaluated five AI assistants and found that matching a user’s views could compete with giving accurate answers. The evaluated models do not represent every current service. A prompt asking for alternatives is not a validated guarantee against sycophancy. [Sharma and colleagues. Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models (ICLR 2024; arXiv revision 2025).](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)

Psychologist Ziva Kunda reviewed research on motivated reasoning. Desired conclusions can shape which reasons people search for and find convincing. Disagreement alone cannot establish a hidden motive, and a desired conclusion can still be correct. [Kunda (1990). The case for motivated reasoning.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2270237/)

## A prompt to adapt {#prompt}

Copy this only after replacing the bracketed parts with information you want to share:

```text
Help me think through an interaction. Treat my account as incomplete.
Observed or reported events: [exact actions, context and source].
My interpretation: [what I currently think].
An exception or missing perspective: [what does not fit or is unknown].
My goal: [understanding, a decision, collaboration or a boundary].
Separate facts in my account, possible explanations and your suggestions.
Give credible alternatives without inventing history or diagnosing anyone.
What information could change the leading explanation?
Suggest one action within my control and what to review afterward.
For research claims, identify a checkable source or say you cannot verify it.
```

This is an editorial aid, not a validated safeguard. In the app, Settings → AI connections selects a provider and model. Sending is optional; preview and redact the selected context first. The book’s practice notes are separate from app notes and are not automatically shared.

## What else could explain it? {#a-distinction-to-keep-in-mind}

A useful suggestion can still rest on an unverified premise. Agreement from several models may reflect similar prompts or training rather than independent corroboration. A provider account or API key establishes access, not scientific reliability, confidentiality or a diagnosis.

These alternatives are starting points, not an exhaustive list.

## What to notice and check

Separate reported events, your interpretations, general research claims and proposed actions. Ask which facts are missing and what would challenge the explanation. Open consequential sources and check whether they support the actual claim. Do not treat a plausible citation, numerical certainty or confident diagnosis as verification.

## How to respond

Share only the context needed and remove unnecessary identifying details. Review the exact text before sending and the provider’s current terms for sensitive data. Ask for alternatives and an action useful under uncertainty; keep output as a suggestion until you decide what, if anything, to adopt. For safety, legal or clinical decisions, use an appropriate qualified route rather than relying on the model’s verdict.

**A brief fictional example:** You replace “My mother-in-law is asserting dominance through names” with the exact introductions, known preferences and an exception. Asking what could distinguish custom, preference and selective disregard produces a better question. It does not guarantee a correct answer.

## Key takeaways {#key-takeaways}

- AI does not add independent observations of your situation.
- Prompt wording and missing context can shape a convincing story.
- Keep suggestions separate, check sources and choose a proportionate next step.

<section class="reader-action compact-practice" markdown="1" aria-labelledby="practice">

## Put it into practice {#practice}

1. Write one AI question without assuming the motive. Separate two observations, your first interpretation and one important unknown.
2. Use the prompt below or the app’s editable preview. Ask for a credible alternative and a useful action that does not depend on a diagnosis.
3. Review one claim against a real source and record what changed in your thinking. Save any useful response as a suggestion, not an event.
