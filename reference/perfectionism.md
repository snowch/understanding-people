# Perfectionism & inflexible standards

Perfectionism concerns demanding standards and the way people judge themselves or others when those standards are not met. A useful distinction is between striving for high quality and being preoccupied with mistakes, criticism or falling short. These can coexist. Careful work is not inherently a problem; rigidity and cost are important.

## How it may arise

A task may become a test of personal worth, safety or acceptance rather than a bounded piece of work. Rechecking can reduce doubt briefly while delaying completion. The setting may also genuinely punish small mistakes. Possible personal and environmental influences should be examined together rather than assuming an internal defect.

## What the evidence supports

Karina Limburg and colleagues combined 284 studies of perfectionism and mental-health outcomes. Perfectionistic concerns and strivings were associated with several difficulties. Associations do not diagnose a disorder or mean that careful work and high standards are inherently harmful. [Limburg and colleagues (2017). The Relationship Between Perfectionism and Psychopathology: A Meta-Analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28026869/)

Christopher Soto and Oliver John developed and tested the BFI-2 across three studies, organising personality descriptions into five broad dimensions and narrower facets. These are dimensions, not fixed types or clinical diagnoses. This entry does not reproduce, administer or score the questionnaire. [Soto & John (2017). The next Big Five Inventory (BFI-2): Developing and assessing a hierarchical model with 15 facets to enhance bandwidth, fidelity, and predictive power.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27055049/)

Sarah Shihata and colleagues reviewed what was known and unresolved about intolerance of uncertainty across emotional disorders. Uncertainty can be difficult without implying a disorder. The review does not establish what level of uncertainty another person should tolerate. [Shihata and colleagues (2016). Intolerance of uncertainty in emotional disorders: What uncertainties remain?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27212227/)

## What else could explain it? {#a-distinction-to-keep-in-mind}

High stakes can justify rigorous checking. An unclear brief can produce repeated revision without perfectionism. Conscientiousness, professional standards, OCD and obsessive-compulsive personality disorder are different concepts. You cannot infer a disorder from a tidy home, demanding work or a preference for a particular method.

These alternatives are starting points, not an exhaustive list.

## What to notice and check

Ask what standard is necessary for the purpose, who set it and what would count as completion. Compare the value of another revision with its cost in time, rest and other responsibilities. Notice whether mistakes lead to a specific correction or a global self-judgment. Include your effect on others when you delegate but reject every acceptable alternative.

## How to respond

Agree observable completion criteria, a proportionate checking process and a stopping point. For a low-risk task, try submitting an adequate version and reviewing the result. Do not remove necessary safeguards in a safety-critical setting. If standards and doubt are causing substantial distress or impairment, professional support may be more useful than another productivity rule.

**A brief fictional example:** Fictional example: You rewrite an ordinary meeting summary five times because it could be better. Agreeing that it must accurately state decisions, owners and dates gives a concrete endpoint. It separates the document’s job from an impossible requirement to eliminate every possible criticism.

## Key takeaways {#key-takeaways}

- High standards and costly self-evaluation are distinguishable.
- Judge the standard against the task’s real purpose.
- A stopping point should preserve necessary safety and quality.

<section class="reader-action compact-practice" markdown="1" aria-labelledby="practice">

## Put it into practice {#practice}

1. Choose one low-risk task and state what adequate completion requires.
2. Use a bounded revision process and finish at the agreed point.
3. Review actual quality, cost and your reaction; do not judge success only by feeling certain.
