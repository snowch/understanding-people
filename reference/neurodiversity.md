# Neurodiversity & communication fit

Neurodiversity concerns variation in how people experience, process and respond to the world. Neurodivergent is an umbrella term used in different ways, often including autism and ADHD. It is not one diagnosis, personality type or fixed list of needs. Ask about the individual rather than inferring needs from the umbrella label.

> **Remember:** Investigate the fit between people and the setting.

The brief example is fictional. Possible explanations and practical suggestions are editorial applications; they are not a test that identifies a motive, trait or diagnosis.

## How it may arise

An interaction can become difficult when sensory conditions, timing, ambiguity or communication expectations do not fit the people involved. The mismatch is not necessarily located in one person alone. A preference for direct wording or preparation time may be useful information about access, not evidence of unwillingness or a lack of care.

## What the evidence supports

Catherine Crompton and colleagues studied story-information transfer in autistic, non-autistic and mixed groups. Transfer and rapport were poorer in mixed groups than within same-neurotype groups in this task. This supports attention to communication fit, not a rule about every autistic person or all forms of neurodivergence. [Crompton and colleagues (2020). Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32431157/)

Susan Michie and colleagues reviewed behaviour-change frameworks and proposed COM-B: capability, opportunity and motivation as interacting conditions for behaviour. They examined its use in tobacco-control and obesity policy. It is a framework for analysing and designing change, not a diagnostic test or proof that one identified barrier caused an action. [Michie, van Stralen & West (2011). The behaviour change wheel: a new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21513547/)

Tal Eyal and colleagues tested interpersonal predictions across a series of experiments. Imagining another person’s perspective did not consistently improve accuracy; a final experiment found that obtaining information through conversation helped. Conversation does not guarantee honesty, complete understanding or safety. These were specific prediction tasks, not tests of every relationship. [Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018). Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/)

## What else could explain it? {#a-distinction-to-keep-in-mind}

Silence, interruption, missed cues and a need for routine can have many explanations. They do not identify a diagnosis, and the same diagnosis does not imply identical abilities or preferences. Neurodivergence and a health difficulty can coexist. Harmful conduct still needs addressing, while accommodations should not depend on proving a moral excuse.

These alternatives are starting points, not an exhaustive list.

## What to notice and check

Ask what is difficult in the actual task: noise, pace, unclear roles, a changing plan or another condition. Compare settings in which communication works better. Use the person’s own account and avoid requiring unnecessary diagnostic disclosure. Notice which expectations are essential to the task and which are simply familiar to you.

## How to respond

Agree a concrete adjustment where possible: clear written decisions, advance information, a quieter setting, breaks or another medium. Review its usefulness with the person rather than treating a label as a complete accommodation plan. A professional or relevant service can help with assessment and support when needed; the book does not supply a diagnostic route from behaviour examples.

**A brief fictional example:** Fictional example: A colleague appears disengaged during noisy brainstorming but contributes clearly when questions arrive in advance. Testing preparation time and a quieter format examines the interaction conditions. It does not establish autism or explain every earlier disagreement.

## Key takeaways {#key-takeaways}

- Neurodiversity is broader than a single diagnosis.
- Communication can depend on the fit between people and conditions.
- Ask about specific access needs and review adjustments.

<section class="reader-action compact-practice" markdown="1" aria-labelledby="practice">

## Put it into practice {#practice}

1. Name one task difficulty and the conditions in which it appears.
2. Agree one wanted adjustment without demanding unnecessary personal disclosure.
3. Review access, contribution and the person’s feedback rather than conformity to one communication style.
