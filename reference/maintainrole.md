# Trying to retain a role

Trying to retain a role is a possible intention to preserve familiar involvement, responsibility or recognition. It can arise when children become adults, a colleague takes over a project, a person retires or care needs change. Continuing an old practice does not prove a strategy to control; it may reflect an unclear handover or a genuinely shared responsibility.

> **Remember:** Clarify what changes and what still matters.

The brief example is fictional. Possible explanations and practical suggestions are editorial applications; they are not a test that identifies a motive, trait or diagnosis.

## How it may arise

A role can provide identity, connection, competence or status as well as practical work. Losing part of it may leave uncertainty about how to contribute. The person taking over may also expect independence without recognising the work that remains. These are possibilities to discuss, not a reason to infer a childhood history or assign blame.

## What the evidence supports

Richard Ryan and Edward Deci reviewed self-determination theory and evidence concerning autonomy, competence and relatedness in motivation and well-being. The framework suggests distinctions to investigate. It cannot identify the cause of a particular action or refusal. [Ryan & Deci (2000). Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/)

Cameron Anderson and colleagues reviewed evidence on the desire for respect, admiration and voluntary deference, distinguishing status from power, money and belonging. A broad motive does not explain every interruption, disagreement or wish for recognition. [Anderson, Hildreth & Howland (2015). Is the desire for status a fundamental human motive? A review of the empirical literature.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25774679/)

Tal Eyal and colleagues tested interpersonal predictions across a series of experiments. Imagining another person’s perspective did not consistently improve accuracy; a final experiment found that obtaining information through conversation helped. Conversation does not guarantee honesty, complete understanding or safety. These were specific prediction tasks, not tests of every relationship. [Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018). Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/)

## What else could explain it? {#a-distinction-to-keep-in-mind}

Role retention differs from expertise, a continuing duty or a concern about an unfinished task. It can also become restrictive when someone refuses agreed changes, withholds information or makes others dependent. Gratitude for past help does not require preserving decision rights forever. A new role should not remove legitimate safeguards merely to demonstrate autonomy.

These alternatives are starting points, not an exhaustive list.

## What to notice and check

Ask what was explicitly agreed about the transition: decisions, tasks, information and accountability. Look for places where expectations differ. Compare what happens when a meaningful contribution is invited without surrendering the new decision-maker’s role. Include your own tendency either to take over or to expect someone else to remain available invisibly.

## How to respond

Make a concrete handover, acknowledge the earlier contribution and agree which support will be requested. Set a time to review what is working. If unwanted involvement continues, state the particular decision or access limit. Where care, employment or legal responsibilities are involved, clarify them with appropriate advice rather than relying on assumptions about family roles.

**A brief fictional example:** Fictional example: A parent keeps booking appointments for an adult child. The child says which bookings they will now manage and asks for help only with transport. They can value the parent’s support while changing who makes the arrangements.

## Key takeaways {#key-takeaways}

- Roles carry practical work and personal meaning.
- A handover needs explicit decisions and responsibilities.
- Appreciation does not require indefinite loss of choice.

<section class="reader-action compact-practice" markdown="1" aria-labelledby="practice">

## Put it into practice {#practice}

1. Identify one changing role and the decisions or tasks that remain unclear.
2. Agree one explicit transfer or requested form of support.
3. Review whether responsibilities and choices became clearer without leaving essential work unowned.
